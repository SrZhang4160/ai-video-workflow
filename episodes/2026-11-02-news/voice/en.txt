Third-party AI agents ordering food on DoorDash
have placed maybe several hundred orders. In total.
DoorDash handled 993 million orders last quarter alone.
The CEO said it on the October 29 earnings call,
naming agents like Meta's Muse, Instinct and Fo.
"Never fight gravity," he said, "but don't be delusional."
In its Q3 letter, DoorDash says it wants to win "the battle for atoms":
warehousing and autonomous delivery. Real things, to your door.
On AI, it built its own agents first:
Ask DoorDash, Text DoorDash, and a command-line agent.
And the CEO floated selling ads inside third-party agents someday.
Why so few? DoorDash didn't say. Two facts help:
One: most people still order in the DoorDash app.
Two: the door for outside agents is narrow.
The MCP connector is for corporate ordering, in a limited beta,
and employees must sign in and accept the terms first.
Retailers are split. Amazon started blocking agents from buying for shoppers.
Walmart opened an MCP gateway instead, with tight rules:
agents can't see your payment info;
each agent gets its own profile, like an authorized card user;
and you can approve every order or set a spending limit.
My take: the plumbing is there. Trust is not.
Platforms will let agents in, but on the platform's terms.
If you build agents, ship approval and spending limits first.
In one line: the road is built; the orders aren't here.
Watch for Walmart's agent profiles rolling out,
and whether DoorDash starts reporting agent orders.
