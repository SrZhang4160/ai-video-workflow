Before a League of Legends match starts,
the two teams take turns banning and picking champions.
This phase is called the draft.
Can A I guess what the other team picks next?
An open-source project on GitHub tried it.
It trained on nearly eighteen hundred pro games,
then tested on about five hundred games from the next four months.
The model never saw the test games during training.
For each move, the model names five candidates.
If the team's real choice is among those five,
that counts as one hit.
This way of scoring is called top five accuracy.
Its score is sixty-two percent.
Is that high or low?
You can't tell from that number alone.
You need a very simple method to compare against.
The simplest one trains no model at all.
It just guesses the most popular champions on the patch.
That alone gets fifty percent.
A simple method used for comparison is called a baseline.
The model beats the baseline by twelve points,
and those twelve points are what the model adds.
So whenever you see an A I accuracy number,
ask first: what's the baseline?
