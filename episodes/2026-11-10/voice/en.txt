Doubling every number with a loop?
With an iterator and a closure, one line does it.
First, closures: a tiny function with no name.
Parameters go between the two bars, the result comes after.
Store it in a variable and call it like a function.
Closures can also use variables from outside.
This k: the closure just uses it.
Try that with a plain fn and it won't compile.
An iterator hands out items one at a time.
map transforms them, filter picks some,
and each step takes a closure.
Then sum finishes it off with the result.
But careful: iterators are lazy.
A map with nothing at the end does nothing at all.
The compiler even warns you about it.
Add collect or sum, and only then does it run.
To sum up: a closure is a tiny nameless function,
an iterator hands out items one by one,
steps in between shape them, and the end makes it run.
