The interviewer asks: how much disk for a year?
Don't panic. Multiply four numbers and you're done.
The whole formula is one line:
new records per day, times size per record,
then times three hundred sixty-five days, times the number of copies.
Example: an app where people post.
ten million daily users, two posts each per day,
so twenty million new posts a day.
One post: the text, plus author and time.
Add indexes and headroom: about one kilobyte.
Twenty million times one kilobyte. How much is that?
Each unit step up is times one thousand.
So about twenty gigabytes a day.
Twenty gigabytes a day, times three hundred sixty-five days,
is about seven point three terabytes a year.
Data is commonly kept in three copies, in case a machine dies.
Times three: about twenty-two terabytes a year.
What if each post also has a two hundred kilobyte image?
That is four terabytes a day, about one point five petabytes a year.
Images dominate. The text barely matters.
Records, times size, times three sixty-five, times copies.
