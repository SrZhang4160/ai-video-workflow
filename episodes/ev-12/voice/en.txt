We have a rubric for judging refund emails,
and now a model uses it to judge each email.
But can we actually trust its verdicts?
If the judge is wrong, every score is wrong.
What many people do is skim a few results,
and if they look right, assume the judge is fine.
But the ones you skim tend to be the easy ones,
and you may never see the hard cases at all.
So you don't really know how accurate it is.
So check it carefully against human judgment.
How? First, sample some reply emails,
with good ones, bad ones, and hard ones too.
Then people who know the refund policy use the same rubric
to mark each item yes or no. Those are human labels.
The judge grades the same batch, and we compare.
The share that match is the agreement rate.
You just divide matching answers by all answers.
The higher it is, the closer the judge is to people.
But don't stop at one total; look at each rubric item.
Why? Suppose most emails are fine to begin with.
Then a judge that passes everything still scores well.
So check whether it catches the ones people failed.
Even more useful is reading where they disagree.
Disagreements usually come from one of three causes.
One is a vague item, read two different ways.
Does "refunded soon" count as stating a time?
Then fix the item to ask for the number of days.
Another is a judge missing the facts,
like whether approval happened. Then give it that.
And sometimes the human was wrong, so fix the label.
After each fix, rerun the judge and compare again.
If an item still disagrees after several rounds,
try a stronger model, or leave that item to people.
And whenever the judge model or rubric changes,
recheck it against the same human labels.
So trust a judge only after checking it against people.
Asked in an interview how you'd calibrate a judge? Say this.
Use human labels as the standard and compare the judge with them.
Measure agreement per rubric item, then read the disagreements.
Change the judge model or the rubric, and recheck.
To start, pull thirty replies,
and have someone who knows the policy label them by the rubric.
