So how does a computer know two texts mean the same thing?
An embedding model turns text into a vector: a list of numbers.
Here's the thing: similar meaning lands close together.
"Refund policy" and "get my money back" share no words. Still close.
And unrelated text, like "cat photos", lands far away.
So how do you measure "close"? A common way: cosine similarity.
It compares two vectors by their angle, not their length.
Same direction: close to one, very similar. Wider angle: lower score.
So retrieval just means: find the chunks with the highest scores.
But why retrieve at all? Why not just ask the model?
Because it's never seen your handbook, or last week's changes.
So rag retrieves the relevant chunks and puts them in the prompt.
Then the model answers from those chunks, and can cite them.
Which means the answer can only be as good as what you retrieved.
Before you retrieve anything, you cut your docs into chunks. How big?
Go too big, and one chunk mixes many topics: a noisy match.
Too small, and you get: "is fifteen days." Fifteen days of what?
A common middle: one idea per chunk, with a little overlap.
There's no universal best size. Try a few, and let your evals decide.
Now, where do all these vectors live? In a vector database.
It stores each vector together with its text and its metadata.
Checking a question against every vector gets slow at scale.
So it uses approximate nearest-neighbor search: close matches, fast.
And metadata filters narrow it down, like: only the twenty twenty-six handbook.
Now say a user asks about error E forty twelve. That code must match exactly.

This is what keyword search, like B M twenty-five, is for: exact, rare terms.
Hybrid search runs both, and merges the two ranked lists.
Search is fast, but rough. So you grab many candidates, say fifty.
Then a reranker reads the question with each chunk, and scores it.
That's slower, but more precise. So it only sees the shortlist.
The top few go into the prompt: less noise, usually better answers.

Retrieval: for each test question, is the right chunk in the top few?
Generation: given the right chunks, does the answer stick to them?
So when a score drops, you know which half to fix.
