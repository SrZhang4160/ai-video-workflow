When we say a system is "fast", what do we mean?
One request done sooner, or more requests per second?
Latency: how long one request waits, in milliseconds.
Throughput: requests finished per second, or Q P S.
Picture the system as a highway.
Latency: one car takes one hour from A to B.
Throughput: how many cars get through each hour.
One lane: about two thousand cars an hour.
Four lanes: about eight thousand. Four times the throughput.
With no traffic jam, each car still takes one hour.
Say one disk write takes about ten milliseconds, for one record or ten.
One record per write: one hundred records a second.
Ten records per write: one thousand a second.
But each record still waits at least ten milliseconds.
Cut latency: cache, move closer, skip a hop.
Raise throughput: more servers, parallel requests, batching.
Sum up: latency asks how long one takes,
throughput asks how many per second. Not the same.
