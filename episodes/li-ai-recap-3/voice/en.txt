Next question: ask the bot the same thing twice. Same answer?
Not always. The model picks each next token by sampling.
And temperature sets how random that pick is.
Low means focused and predictable. High means more varied.
Now, a lot of people think temperature zero means identical.
It's more repeatable, but it's not guaranteed to be identical.
Top P cuts unlikely tokens. Tune one, not both; some models lock them.
That matters for evals: if answers vary, one run won't tell you much.
Now, what if the answer just stops, right in the middle of a sentence?
Usually that's max tokens. It caps how long the answer can be.
So don't guess. Check the stop reason that comes back with it.
Each provider names it a bit differently, so check the docs.
If it says max tokens, or length, the answer was cut off.
So raise the limit, or ask for a shorter answer.
You've seen chat apps type the answer out, word by word. How?
That's streaming. Without it, you wait for the whole answer.
With it, chunks arrive as they're made, over server-sent events.
Your app shows each chunk right away. Same total time, feels faster.

Here's one people miss: output tokens usually cost more. Why?
The input is read in parallel, all at once.
