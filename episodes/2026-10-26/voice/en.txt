In Rust, every value has a type.
The basic types come in two groups:
scalar and compound.
Integers first: i means signed,
u means unsigned, zero or more.
The number after it is the size in bits.
Leave the type out and you get i thirty-two.
Numbers with a decimal point are floats: f sixty-four.
Careful: you can't add an integer to a float,
Rust won't convert it for you.
A bool is just true or false.
A char uses single quotes: one character,
and it can hold Chinese or even an emoji.
First compound type: the tuple.
It packs values of different types together,
and you read them by position: t dot zero, t dot one.
An array needs every element to be the same type,
and its length is fixed, written in the type.
Read items with square brackets.
To recap: a scalar is a single value,
a tuple holds mixed types,
an array holds just one type.
