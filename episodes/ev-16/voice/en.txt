This wraps up the Metrics block.
Say the refund agent has versions A and B.
Both pass ninety percent of the same eval.
They look equal, but in production they can differ a lot.
So watch four more things beyond success rate.
They decide how well it works once it's live.
First, cost, meaning how many tokens it uses.
Models charge per token, so more tokens cost more.
Say A uses three thousand tokens per refund,
and B uses twelve thousand, four times the cost.
At high volume, that gap is real money.
Next, latency: how long the customer waits for a reply.
Say A answers in ten seconds; B takes forty.
Wait too long, and the customer may just leave.
Then come steps and tool calls.
Each time the model thinks or calls a tool, that's one step.
A normal refund needs three tool calls:
look up the order, refund, then send an email.
But B looked up the same order four times.
More steps mean more tokens and more time.
Extra steps often mean it's looping or retrying.
Then read its step-by-step log to find out why.
Last, and most important: safety violations.
The rule says refunds over five hundred dollars need a human's approval.
If it refunds eight hundred dollars without that approval,
that's a violation, even if the customer is happy.
Not checking the buyer owns the order counts too.
Count these separately, not inside the success rate.
Otherwise a ninety percent success rate hides it.
Yet that one case can mean real money lost.
Aim for zero; your team can even block release on one.
Back to A and B: put the five numbers side by side.
Same success, but A is cheaper, faster, with fewer steps.
If A also has zero violations, pick A.
The core: at equal success, pick the cheaper, faster, safer one.
Success rate is the bar; past it, compare the rest.
After every change, measure them all again.
Done by hand, sooner or later it gets skipped.
