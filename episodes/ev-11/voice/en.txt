Still grading, but now the part code can't check.
After the refund, the agent emails the customer.
Code can check it was sent, not how it reads.
That part goes to a language model as the judge.
That's an L L M judge. So how do you write its rubric?
The lazy way is to just ask it to score the email.
"From one to ten, how good is this reply?"
But what counts as good? The prompt never says.
The same email gets a seven, then an eight.
And a low score doesn't say what was wrong.
So the score is unstable and doesn't tell you what to fix.
So split the rubric into specific questions.
Each item asks one thing, answered yes or no.
For the refund email, it could look like this.
First, does the email state the refund amount,
and does it match the order?
Does it say roughly when the money arrives?
Customers care about those two the most.
The most important one: does it promise anything off-policy?
For example, promising a refund after thirty days.
Or promising a refund over five hundred dollars before approval.
Once said, the shop must honor it or apologize.
And last, is the tone polite and friendly?
"Polite" is vague, so spell out what it means,
like apologizing first and never blaming the customer.
Each of these is a clear yes or no.
Items alone aren't enough; the judge needs facts.
So give it the order amount and refund policy.
Otherwise it can't tell if the amount is right.
Have it write a reason before each yes or no.
If it gets one wrong, you can see why.
If facts are missing, let it say "unknown,"
so it doesn't guess just to give an answer.
It passes only if every item is yes.
And any no tells you exactly what to fix.
Core: break the rubric into concrete yes or no items.
And only open-ended replies go to the model judge.
But how do you know the judge itself is right?
