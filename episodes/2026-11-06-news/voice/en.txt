Make a reasoning model think almost half as much, and still get it right.
On AIME24 math, thinking drops from 13,493 to 7,364 tokens.
Accuracy didn't drop. It even went up a little.
That is what a new Tencent and Xi'an Jiaotong paper reports.
The paper is called LoCo: "Think Less, Locally."
It made Hugging Face Daily Papers, with 56 upvotes when we looked.
Reasoning models overthink: they have the answer and keep paying to think.
LoCo first finds the step where overthinking starts.
It keeps everything before it, and regenerates 8 times from there,
keeping the shortest one that is still correct.
Then DPO training teaches it to take the shorter path from that step.
How? It looks at how confident the model is at each step.
Steps with low, jumpy confidence go to a judge model to confirm.
In the table, another method also gets shorter, but loses accuracy.
Authors say: 40%+ fewer tokens on math, 23–36% fewer on code.
Want to try it? The LoCo-7B weights are on Hugging Face,
MIT licensed, code on GitHub, and it runs with vLLM.
The minimum GPU isn't stated; the authors used NVIDIA H20s.
My take: only one 7B model was tested,
the numbers are the authors' own, and AIME scores swing a lot,
and it works by changing the weights, so closed API models can't use it.
In one line: don't cut thinking everywhere. Fix the step where it overthinks.
What to watch: whether others reproduce it on other models.
