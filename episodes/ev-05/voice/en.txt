We're still on test cases, now ones other people wrote.
Every new model launches with a table of scores.
Those scores come from public benchmarks.
A benchmark is a public set of tasks plus grading rules.
Since the tasks are public, anyone can run them and compare.
With a high score, will your refund agent work well?
Not necessarily. Let's first see what each one tests.
S W E bench tests fixing code.
Its tasks are real bugs from open-source projects.
A fix counts only if the project's tests pass.
A human-checked subset is called Verified.
tau bench tests customer service, closest to ours.
A model plays the customer, with a mock database behind it.
At the end, it checks whether the database ended up right.
Web Arena tests doing tasks on websites,
O S World tests using apps on a computer,
Gaia tests answering questions with search and tools,
and B F C L tests whether function calls are correct.
Each has its own focus, but they all test general skills.
So why doesn't a high score carry over?
First, data contamination. Benchmark tasks are public,
and models are trained on huge amounts of web text.
So tasks and answers can leak into training data.
The fixes for those coding tasks, for example, are online.
The model may have memorized answers, not learned the skill.
So the score comes out higher than its real ability.
Then distribution mismatch: their tasks don't look like yours.
Your policy allows refunds only within thirty days,
over five hundred dollars needs a human's approval,
and you verify the customer owns the order.
Those are your own rules, and no benchmark tests them.
Yet they're exactly what your agent deals with every day.
Even tau bench's retail tasks use a different policy.
So what are benchmark scores for? Shortlisting models.
Models that score clearly lower can be dropped early.
But to pick one, or to check whether a change helped,
you have to measure on your own eval set, case by case.
Its tasks come from your refund chats, graded by your rules.
The core: benchmarks show general skill; your tests show fit.
