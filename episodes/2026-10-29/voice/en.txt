Rust has no garbage collector,
and you don't free memory by hand.
So who cleans up? Three simple rules.
Rule one: every value has an owner.
Here, the owner of this string is s.
Rule two: only one owner at a time.
Assign s one to s two, and the owner changes.
Use s one again, and the compiler says no.
Rule three: when the owner goes out of scope,
the value is dropped and its memory freed.
The compiler inserts that cleanup for you.
Compare: manual free is easy to forget or double up,
a garbage collector costs time at runtime,
ownership settles it all at compile time.
To sum up: one value, one owner,
and when the owner leaves, the value goes too.
