With generics, one copy is enough.
Put angle bracket T after the function name.
T is a type parameter: it stands for "some type".
When you call it, the compiler infers what T is.
Back to max: can we just write a greater than b?
It won't compile: T might not be comparable.
Add the bound T colon PartialOrd, and it works.
That's what last lesson's traits are for.
Do generics slow your code down? No.
At compile time, Rust makes a copy for each type you use.
So it runs as fast as hand-written code.
Option of T and Vec of T are already generic.
Your own structs can be generic too.
To recap: angle bracket T declares a type parameter,
a trait bound says what T can do,
and it expands at compile time, with no extra cost.
