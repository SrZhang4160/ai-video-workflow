People often ask: can A I predict who wins a match?
One open-source project that analyzes drafts
has an answer: it gives no win rate.
It's not that it didn't try.
It scored each team's draft from several angles.
How common the champions are in pro play,
how much the players have used them,
and whether they are strong in lane.
Then it checked those scores against finished games,
to see if the higher-scoring side really won.
The measure it used is called A U C.
An A U C of zero point five is the same as flipping a coin.
Every angle came out close to zero point five.
No single angle could tell winners from losers.
The project offers two likely reasons.
One: the gap in strength between the two teams
matters far more than the draft does.
Two: both sides pick to counter each other,
so most of the effects cancel out.
Forcing out a win rate at this point
gives a number that looks precise but has nothing behind it.
So it only lays out the comparison for each angle.
No combined score, and no win rate.
A model that admits it can't tell
is more trustworthy than one that hands you a number.
